Fixed-fee scope creep: a template to stop unpaid “quick” revisions

Scope creep rarely arrives as a villain speech. It shows up as “quick,” “small,” and “while you’re in there.” If you sell UI/product design on a fixed fee, every unclear yes is a silent discount. This page is a scope creep checklist + 72-hour fix — then a clear path to the full Change Request kit if you want it.

If this sounds familiar

  • “One more screen” lands after the revision cap — and you still open Figma.

12 red flags (sales → kickoff → delivery)

Sales & fit

  • 1. No budget range, only “flexible.”
  • 2. Committee with no named decider.
  • 3. “We’ll know it when we see it.”
  • 4. Rush + undefined scope.

Kickoff

  • 5. Deposit skipped “because we’re friends.”
  • 6. Assets “coming soon” with a hard launch date.
  • 7. Feedback in four channels at once.
  • 8. Unlimited revisions promised on the sales call.

Delivery

  • 9. A “tweak” that needs new modules or pages → change order, not a revision.
  • 10. New stakeholder at week 3 rewriting goals.
  • 11. Revision cap hit, still “just polish.”
  • 12. Final files before final payment.

72-hour fix (do this once)

1 Day 1 — Write the defaults

Put your default revision cap and deposit % on a sticky (or a Notion page). If it isn’t written, you’ll negotiate against yourself under pressure.

2 Day 2 — Stand up a Change Request list

Even a simple table works: ask · in/out of scope · quote · status. Every new ask gets a row before you open Figma again.

3 Day 3 — Save two scripts

A change-order email and a revision-boundary reply. Short, priced, optional — not a guilt essay.

What “priced and optional” looks like

New work stays optional when it’s visible and priced — not buried in a goodwill Slack thread. A Change Request row is often enough; you don’t need a heavier CRM to defend a fixed fee.

This is process, not legal advice. Attach your own contract/MSA when you need one.
