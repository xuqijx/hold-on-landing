Fixed-fee scope defense

Scope creep on fixed-fee UI work — stop unpaid “quick” revisions

On fixed-price UI and product design work, margin dies in the inbox: “Can you just tweak this?” “One more screen.” “The stakeholder changed their mind.” None of those asks are free if they sit outside the signed scope — but without a written capture habit, they feel free. Scope creep is unpaid work that never got a change request.

Copyable change-order note

Paste this into email. Fill the brackets. It records an offer. It is not a contract and it is not legal advice.

Subject: Change request — [one-line ask]

Hi [name],

You asked for [one sentence] on [date].
Against the current scope, this is out of scope.

Option A — change order
Fee: [amount]
Adds: [deliverable]
Timing: [date or number of days after you confirm]
Does not include: [boundary]

Option B — later phase
I can leave it out of this round and quote it separately.

I will start only after you reply with a yes and the fee is agreed.
If you would rather drop it, that is fine too.

Thanks,
[your name]

What counts as scope creep

Anything that expands effort after the fee and deliverables were agreed — and is treated as “included” without a new price or timeline.

Common patterns on fixed-fee Figma→handoff or product UI packages:

  • Extra screens, states, or breakpoints not listed in the SOW
  • New stakeholder rounds after the agreed review window
  • Brand pivots mid-build (“use the new logo set”) without a change order
  • “Quick” copy, icon, or layout passes that stack into hours
  • Re-opening closed acceptance with a fresh wish list

Hourly shops bill the clock. Fixed-fee shops must name the ask, or they donate the hours.

Capture every change request

Do not argue in Slack threads alone. Every new ask gets a Change Request row before you design:

  1. Who asked and when
  2. What they want in one plain sentence
  3. In scope / out of scope vs the signed SOW
  4. Impact — hours estimate or “TBD after 15-min review”
  5. Decision — absorb (rare, written), change order, or decline

A Change Request log is not a CRM. It is a ledger so “quick” never means invisible. If it is not in the log, it did not happen for billing purposes.

Boundary scripts at onboarding

Boundaries land best before the first unpaid revision — at kickoff and deposit.

Cover in writing (email or kickoff doc):

  • What the fixed fee includes (screens, rounds, formats)
  • How many review rounds and who the decision-maker is
  • How new asks are logged and priced
  • What “done” / acceptance looks like

A short script beats a long lecture: “Happy to explore that — I’ll log it as a change request and send options (absorb / change order / later phase) within one business day.” Clients who hate that sentence are telling you how the project will feel.

Upstream, a clear discovery→proposal handoff reduces surprise asks later. If you sell discovery separately, keep it linked to paid scope — see Discovery→Proposal Kit.

Weekly CR review

Once a week, open the Change Request view — not your entire Notion workspace.

  • Anything open older than 48 hours: decide absorb / change order / decline
  • Anything approved without a new fee or date: fix the paperwork before you draw
  • Anything absorbed: count it so “goodwill” does not become the default

A short weekly pass protects more margin than another late polish.

When to send a change order

Send a change order when the ask is out of scope and you are willing to do the work for a defined fee and timeline.

Use a change order (not a vague “sure”) when:

  • Effort is more than a trivial tweak (your threshold — for example, more than 30–60 minutes)
  • It adds screens, flows, or platforms
  • It reopens a closed milestone
  • A new stakeholder invalidates prior approvals

Decline or park for a later phase when the ask breaks the deadline or the relationship pattern is one-way. Absorb only when you choose to — and still log it.

FAQ

Is logging change requests confrontational?

No. It is operational clarity. Most clients prefer a numbered option over silent resentment and slipped dates.

What if the client says everything is “in spirit” of the SOW?

Point to the written deliverables and rounds. If the SOW is vague, fix the SOW template on the next project — and still price this ask now.

Do I need a lawyer for every change?

This page is not legal advice and does not say a change-order email is legally sufficient. It is a written record of the ask, the fee, and the timing you offered. Whether you also need a contract amendment or counsel depends on your agreement and where you work.

Will this guarantee higher income?

No. It is a workflow to stop unpaid work. Outcomes depend on your rates, clients, and follow-through.
